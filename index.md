### Harvard University | Ph.D. Researcher | 2019-2025 {#hekstra-cv}
My graduate work in Prof. Doeke Hekstra's lab had two major components:

##### Investigated DHFR function, structure, and dynamics
The structure of the enzyme dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) is highly conserved across the tree of life. However, the **dynamics** of DHFR have evolved divergently to meet the metabolic needs of different organisms. I leveraged evolutionary information to engineer a bacterialized, gain-of-function mutant of the human enzyme. I then characterized the conformational dynamics responsible for the function of both *wild-type* and gain-of-function enzymes.
 - I implemented and optimized the entire project workflow from gene, to purified protein, to high-resolution crystal structure.
 - I integrated techniques such as melting (NanoDSF), binding (MST), kinetics, and inhibition assays to characterize substrate and product binding throughout the enzymatic cycle.
 - I designed a custom series of molecular dynamics simulations in OpenMM to show that cofactor oxidation state triggers a long-range conformational change.
 - I trained and mentored rotation students and undergraduates for experimental and computational projects

##### Developed crystallographic methods and software
The Hekstra lab is excited about expanding the capabalities of protein crystallography to characterize protein conformational dynamics, rather than just static structures.
 - I developed a novel algorithm and python package, *matchmaps*, for computing difference maps between pairs of non-isomorphous or poorly isomorphous datasets. This approach has applications in areas such as multi-temperature and multi-pressure crystallography, where an interesting conformational change may be obscured by unit-cell changes.
 - I contributed to the development and application of two perturbative crystallography experiments: multi-temperature crystallography (MT-X), and electric-field stimulated crystallography (EF-X). These techniques expand upon traditional crystallography to allow structural characterization of protein conformation under the influence of some perturbation, either a change in temperature or a strong electric-field pulse.
 - I am a founding "1/astronaut" of the Reciprocal Space Station consortium of open-source software for crystallography. In addition to contributing *matchmaps* to RSS, I have also contributed to development of *reciprocalspaceship*, a python package which combines the strengths of *gemmi* and *pandas* to provide a convenient python API for manipulating diffraction data.
